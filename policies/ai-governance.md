1. PURPOSE

   This policy establishes the governance framework for artificial
   intelligence systems and the technical controls for inference integrity
   within the Axiom Sovereign Engine environment. It addresses both the
   automated cryptographic controls that ensure non-repudiation of AI
   inference activity and the organizational governance structures that
   ensure responsible, accountable, and transparent use of AI.

   This is a mixed-family policy covering:

       Technical Controls (AI-1.x, AI-2.x)
           Automated inference provenance and integrity verification,
           enforced through the SWT3 Witness Anchor protocol.

       Attestation Controls (AI-3.x, AI-4.x)
           Organizational governance, inventory, acceptable use, review
           processes, incident response, and data governance for AI systems.


2. SCOPE

   This policy applies to all AI and machine learning systems that are
   developed, deployed, procured, or operated within or on behalf of the
   Axiom Sovereign Engine environment. This includes:

       a) Large language models (LLMs) used in production or development
       b) Machine learning models performing inference on CUI or sensitive data
       c) AI-assisted development tools (see also SA-15)
       d) Third-party AI APIs and services
       e) Embedded AI capabilities in procured software
       f) All clearing levels (0-Analytics, 1-Standard, 2-Sensitive,
          3-Classified)


3. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

   AI Governance Board
       - Approves addition of new AI models to the inventory
       - Reviews and approves the AI Risk Management Framework annually
       - Adjudicates AI incidents escalated from security operations
       - Approves clearing level assignments for AI use cases

   Information System Security Officer (ISSO)
       - Maintains the AI model inventory (AI-3.2)
       - Reviews SWT3 Witness Anchor logs for anomalies
       - Coordinates AI incident response activities
       - Ensures inference provenance controls are operational

   AI Model Owner (per model)
       - Responsible for risk classification of their model
       - Maintains model documentation (version, training data provenance,
         intended use, known limitations)
       - Reports model drift, bias, or failure events to the ISSO
       - Ensures human-in-the-loop review for high-impact decisions

   System Administrator
       - Maintains technical infrastructure for SWT3 witnessing
       - Monitors latency thresholds and token usage metrics
       - Ensures witness anchor minting is operational across all endpoints

   All Users of AI Systems
       - Comply with the AI Acceptable Use Policy
       - Report suspected AI failures, bias, or misuse
       - Do not use unauthorized AI models or services (shadow AI)


4. POLICY STATEMENTS

   PART A: TECHNICAL INFERENCE CONTROLS
   _____________________________________

   4.1  Inference Provenance (AI-1)

        Every AI inference processed within the authorization boundary is
        subject to cryptographic provenance controls to ensure non-repudiation
        and auditability.

        AI-1.1  Prompt Hashing (SI-7, MEASURE 2.6)

                All prompts submitted to AI models are hashed using SHA-256
                prior to transmission. The hash is recorded as part of the
                SWT3 Witness Anchor, ensuring that the exact input can be
                verified without exposing the content at higher clearing
                levels. This supports integrity verification per SI-7 and
                measurement traceability per AI RMF MEASURE 2.6.

        AI-1.2  Response Anchoring (SI-7, MEASURE 2.6)

                All responses generated by AI models are hashed using SHA-256
                upon receipt. The response hash is recorded alongside the
                prompt hash in the SWT3 Witness Anchor, creating a complete
                cryptographic chain from input to output. Modifications to
                responses after anchoring are detectable.

        AI-1.3  Model Identifier Recorded (AU-3, MAP 3.5)

                The model name and version are captured and recorded with
                every inference event. This supports audit record content
                requirements per AU-3 and model mapping per AI RMF MAP 3.5.
                Model identifiers must be specific (e.g., "gpt-4o-2024-08-06")
                rather than generic aliases.

   4.2  Inference Logging (AI-2)

        Every AI inference event generates a complete audit record through
        the SWT3 Witness Anchor protocol.

        AI-2.1  Witness Anchor Minting (AU-2, GOVERN 1.7)

                A SWT3 Witness Anchor is minted for every inference event.
                The anchor captures: tenant, procedure, factors (prompt hash,
                response hash, model identifier), timestamp, clearing level,
                and computed fingerprint. Anchors are immutable once minted
                and are subject to Merkle tree rollup for tamper evidence.

        AI-2.2  Latency Threshold (SI-4, MEASURE 2.5)

                Inference latency is monitored and recorded as factor_a in
                the witness anchor. The maximum acceptable latency threshold
                is 30,000 milliseconds. Inferences exceeding this threshold
                are flagged for review. Sustained threshold violations
                trigger investigation per SI-4 monitoring requirements.

        AI-2.3  Token Usage Tracking (AU-3, MEASURE 2.5)

                Input and output token counts are recorded for every
                inference event. Token consumption supports cost attribution,
                anomaly detection (unusual token volumes may indicate prompt
                injection or data exfiltration attempts), and capacity
                planning. Token metrics are available through the AI Witness
                dashboard and export APIs.


   PART B: ORGANIZATIONAL AI GOVERNANCE
   ______________________________________

   4.3  AI Risk Management Framework (AI-3.1, PL-2, GOVERN 1.1)

        The organization maintains an AI Risk Management Framework aligned
        with the NIST AI RMF (AI 100-1). The framework addresses all four
        core functions:

            GOVERN    Policies, roles, accountability structures, and
                      organizational commitments for trustworthy AI.

            MAP       Context establishment, stakeholder identification,
                      risk framing for each AI use case.

            MEASURE   Metrics, monitoring, and assessment methods for
                      AI risk and performance.

            MANAGE    Risk response, mitigation actions, and continuous
                      improvement processes.

        The AI Risk Management Framework is reviewed and updated annually
        or upon significant changes to the organization's AI portfolio,
        regulatory environment, or risk posture.

   4.4  AI Model Inventory (AI-3.2, CM-8, GOVERN 1.5)

        All AI models in use within the authorization boundary are registered
        in the AI Model Inventory. The inventory is reviewed quarterly
        (every 90 days) and includes, for each model:

            a) Model name and version
            b) Provider or source
            c) Risk classification (low, moderate, high, critical)
            d) Designated model owner
            e) Authorized use cases and clearing levels
            f) Known limitations and bias considerations
            g) Date added and last review date

        Models not present in the inventory are considered unauthorized.
        Use of unregistered models constitutes a policy violation subject
        to the enforcement provisions in Section 6.

   4.5  AI Acceptable Use Policy (AI-3.3, PL-4, GOVERN 2.1)

        An AI Acceptable Use Policy is established and communicated to all
        personnel. The policy defines:

            a) Which AI models and services are authorized for use
            b) Approved use cases and prohibited activities
            c) Data classification restrictions (what data may be submitted
               to which models at which clearing levels)
            d) Disclosure requirements when AI-generated content is used
               in deliverables or decisions
            e) Prohibitions on submitting CUI to unauthorized external
               AI services
            f) Obligations to report suspected AI misuse or shadow AI

        The AI Acceptable Use Policy is reviewed annually. All personnel
        acknowledge the policy before being granted access to AI systems.

   4.6  AI Output Review (AI-4.1, SI-4, MANAGE 2.2)

        A human-in-the-loop review process is required for AI outputs that
        inform high-impact decisions. High-impact decisions include but are
        not limited to:

            a) Access control determinations
            b) Risk assessment conclusions
            c) Compliance verdicts or audit findings
            d) Financial or procurement recommendations
            e) Personnel actions or evaluations

        The review process is documented, including reviewer qualifications,
        review criteria, and escalation procedures. The AI Output Review
        process is evaluated every 180 days to ensure adequacy.

   4.7  AI Incident Response (AI-4.2, IR-4, MANAGE 4.1)

        AI-specific incident types are incorporated into the organizational
        incident response plan. AI incidents include but are not limited to:

            a) Shadow AI detection (unauthorized model usage)
            b) Model failure modes (hallucination, refusal, degraded output)
            c) Bias incidents (discriminatory or inequitable outputs)
            d) Prompt injection or adversarial input attacks
            e) Data leakage through AI channels
            f) Model drift exceeding acceptable thresholds

        AI incidents follow the standard incident response lifecycle
        (preparation, detection, analysis, containment, eradication,
        recovery, post-incident activity) with AI-specific playbooks
        supplementing the main IR policy.

        The AI Incident Response procedures are reviewed annually.

   4.8  AI Data Governance (AI-4.3, SI-12, MAP 2.3)

        Data governance controls apply to all data used in AI operations,
        including training data, inference inputs, and inference outputs.

            a) Training data provenance is documented for all internally
               developed models, including source, consent basis, and
               known quality issues.

            b) Inference inputs are classified according to clearing levels
               (0 through 3) and processed only by models authorized for
               the applicable clearing level.

            c) Personally identifiable information (PII) in inference
               inputs is handled per the SWT3 clearing level protocol:
               Level 0 (Analytics) retains full content, Level 1 (Standard)
               hashes sensitive fields, Level 2 (Sensitive) redacts PII,
               Level 3 (Classified) redacts all content.

            d) Inference outputs containing PII or CUI are subject to the
               same handling and marking requirements as the input data.

        The AI Data Governance provisions are reviewed annually.

6. ENFORCEMENT

   Violations of technical inference controls (AI-1.x, AI-2.x) are
   detected automatically and result in FAIL verdicts in the SWT3 Witness
   Anchor ledger. Sustained failures trigger investigation and may result
   in model suspension pending remediation.

   Violations of organizational governance controls (AI-3.x, AI-4.x)
   include but are not limited to: use of unauthorized AI models (shadow AI),
   submission of CUI to unapproved services, failure to conduct required
   reviews, and failure to report AI incidents. Violations are subject to
   disciplinary action commensurate with the severity and may result in
   revocation of AI system access.

   Revocation of individual SWT3 Witness Anchors is supported through the
   AI-REV.1 procedure for cases including model recall, policy violation,
   data contamination, consent withdrawal, regulatory order, or error
   correction.
